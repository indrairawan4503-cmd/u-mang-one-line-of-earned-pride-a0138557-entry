# VERSIONS — variant B: "Again."

One concept, iterated v1–v8: the night-shift cleaner who taught herself
to read, reading a bedtime story aloud to her daughter for the first time.
Char counts exclude the trailing newline. Final: v8 (229 chars).

## v1 — 190 chars
> For three years she sounded out the labels on her cleaning cart after her night shift; tonight Rosa reads her daughter a bedtime story all the way to the last page, and doesn't stumble once.

**Kept the concept, cut the landing.** "Doesn't stumble once" reports
success instead of showing it, and the semicolon makes the line feel
assembled, not read in one breath.

## v2 — 194 chars
> She spent three years sounding out the labels on her cleaning cart after her night shift, and tonight Rosa reads her daughter a bedtime story all the way to the last page without stumbling once.

**Cut.** Same weak ending as v1; "without stumbling once" is a claim
about the reading, not a moment the reader can see.

## v3 — 174 chars
> After three years of sounding out the labels on her cleaning cart between night shifts, Rosa reads her daughter a bedtime story, turning the last page without stumbling once.

**Cut.** Tighter, but still ends on an assertion ("without stumbling
once") and "cleaning cart" is a flatter detail than the labels themselves.

## v4 — 178 chars
> After three years of sounding out the words on the spray bottles between night shifts, Rosa reads her little girl a bedtime story, and turns the last page without stumbling once.

**Kept the detail, cut the ending.** "Words on the spray bottles" is the
fresh, specific image this concept needs — she learned to read from
cleaning-product labels. But the landing is still an assertion, and
"little girl" is softer than "daughter".

## v5 — 196 chars
> For three years she sounded out the words on the spray bottles between night shifts; tonight Rosa reads her daughter a bedtime story, and her voice doesn't falter once — not even on the last page.

**Cut.** The em dash tries for a landing but "doesn't falter" is still the
narrator telling the reader the reading went well, and the semicolon
breaks the one-read flow.

## v6 — 195 chars
> After three years of sounding out the words on the spray bottles between night shifts, Rosa reads her daughter a bedtime story aloud, and when the last page turns, her daughter whispers, 'Again.'

**Kept the turn, cut the setup.** The daughter whispering "Again." is the
landing this line was missing: the child's request is the proof the
reading worked, and the pride arrives in the last word. But the opening
is flat ("After three years of...") and there is no image of Rosa herself
in the scene.

## v7 — 224 chars
> She spent three years sounding out the words on the spray bottles between night shifts, and tonight, still in her uniform, Rosa reads her daughter a bedtime story, and when the last page turns her daughter whispers, 'Again.'

**Kept almost everything, cut one phrase.** "Still in her uniform" puts
Rosa in the scene, straight off her shift — the effort is visible on her.
But "and when the last page turns" spends words restating what "Again."
already implies.

## v8 (FINAL) — 229 chars
> She spent three years sounding out the words on the spray bottles between night shifts, and tonight, still in her uniform, Rosa reads her daughter a bedtime story all the way to the last page — and her daughter whispers, 'Again.'

**Chosen.** Keeps v7's scene ("still in her uniform"), the spray-bottle
detail, and the "Again." landing; "all the way to the last page" earns the
turn by showing the reading completed, and the em dash makes the pivot to
the daughter's whisper land in one read, on the last word. 229 chars —
inside the 240 cap with margin, near the top of the byte budget.
